# Brief guidance

Use `APP_BRIEF.example.md` as the structure. Remove instructional text and irrelevant fields. Use `Unknown` only for important expected information.

## Completion conditions

Promotion requires:

- identity and public display name;
- every supplied app source directory reconciled through a matching codebase artifact or recorded with a specific rejection or failure reason;
- release mode;
- proposition, audience, problem, and site goal;
- key features;
- target-app icon or recorded provisional first-party artwork;
- hero-media decision, including a placeholder and replacement brief when needed;
- primary CTA kind, label, and verified destination, or the unreleased disabled-badge default;
- privacy and terms disposition under the legal contract;
- legacy-site relationship, migration decisions, and any required support destination;
- every artifact created or successfully refreshed in the current run listed as consumed or rejected for a specific reason after all retries finish;
- an exact user-supplied accent or a completed visual brand-color resolution.

Ask only when one of these cannot be derived, safely defaulted, or recorded as a truthful provisional decision. Ratings, reviews, production screenshots, final pricing, and release date may remain unknown when they are not needed for an accurate page.

## Recording rules

- Keep facts concise and source material where future verification matters.
- Preserve current explicit user decisions over source alternatives.
- Distinguish observation, attributed claim, inference, conflict, and generated copy.
- Record customer language only with its source and context. A developer slogan or one comment is not representative customer language.
- Record visitor awareness only when known and useful to messaging.
- Provenance belongs in the brief and artifacts, not automatically in public copy.
- A primary feature without publication-ready media remains supported. Give it a provisional or placeholder slot and capture handoff rather than dropping the feature.
- Reconcile first-party privacy wording with App Store disclosure without collapsing distinct data categories.
